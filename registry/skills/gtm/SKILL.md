# Go-to-Market

GTM is how customers find and buy your product. The right approach depends entirely on your product, price point, and customer. There is no universal playbook.

**Pre-PMF rule:** You don't need a "GTM strategy." You need to get the product in front of users however you can and see if they stick. Strategy is for after you know it works.

## Models

### Product-led growth (PLG)

The product sells itself. Users sign up, experience value, and convert to paid.

**Works when:**
- The product can deliver value without human help (self-serve)
- Low price point ($0–$500/mo) where a sales call doesn't make economic sense
- End users have authority to adopt (developers, designers, individual contributors)

**Key metrics:**
- Signup → activation rate (did they reach the "aha" moment?)
- Time-to-value (how fast do they get value? Hours, not days)
- Free → paid conversion rate
- Expansion revenue (users who upgrade or add seats)

**What to focus on:**
- Onboarding. The first 5 minutes determine everything. Remove every step that isn't essential.
- Activation. Define the moment users get real value. Measure how many reach it. Optimize relentlessly.
- Virality. Can users invite others naturally? Can their output be seen by non-users?

### Sales-led

A human sells the product. Demos, calls, contracts.

**Works when:**
- High price point ($10K+/year) where the deal size justifies the sales cost
- The buyer is different from the user (buying committee, procurement)
- The product requires configuration, integration, or customization

**Key metrics:**
- Pipeline coverage (3-4x of target)
- Win rate
- Average deal size (ACV)
- Sales cycle length
- CAC payback period

**What to focus on:**
- ICP definition. Be ruthlessly specific about who you sell to. "Mid-market" is not an ICP. "Series B fintech companies with 50-200 engineers and no dedicated platform team" is.
- Qualification. Kill bad deals early. Time spent on deals you'll lose is the biggest sales team killer.
- Repeatability. Can a second salesperson close deals the way the founder can? If not, you don't have a sales process yet.

### Community-led

Build an audience or community first, then convert to customers.

**Works when:**
- Your users have a shared identity or practice (developers, designers, data scientists)
- You can create genuine value through content, tools, or connection
- Long buying cycles where trust matters more than features

**What to focus on:**
- Give value first. Open-source tools, educational content, free resources. Earn attention, don't buy it.
- Let the community shape the product. The feedback loop is the advantage.
- Be patient. This compounds slowly but creates durable moats.

## Channel selection

### How to pick

1. **Where are your customers already?** Don't drag them to a new place. Go where they are.
2. **What's the CAC math?** If your ACV is $1K/year, you can't afford enterprise sales reps. If it's $100K/year, you can't rely on self-serve.
3. **What's working now?** Look at how your first 10 customers found you. Do more of that.

### Common channels (and when they work)

- **Outbound email/cold outreach:** Works for B2B with clear ICP. Doesn't work if your ICP is vague or your value prop requires education.
- **Content/SEO:** Works when customers search for solutions. Slow to build, durable once it compounds.
- **Word of mouth:** The best channel. You can't force it — it's a signal that your product is working.
- **Partnerships/integrations:** Works after you have a product people use. Don't try to partner your way to PMF.
- **Paid ads:** Works to accelerate something already working organically. Never use paid to mask a product problem.

## Stage-appropriate advice

### 0-10 customers
Do everything manually. Personally recruit every user. The goal is learning, not scaling.

### 10-100 customers
Find the repeatable motion. What type of customer converts best? What message resonates? What channel works? Double down on what's working, cut what isn't.

### 100-1000 customers
Now you can invest in process. Hire your first GTM person. Build the infrastructure (CRM, analytics, content). But only for the motion that's already proven.

## Common mistakes

- **Building a sales team before PMF.** Sales can close deals, but they can't fix a product that doesn't work.
- **Trying all channels at once.** Pick one or two, go deep. Spreading thin across five channels means being mediocre at all of them.
- **Hiring a "head of marketing" too early.** The founder needs to do the first GTM motion themselves to understand what works.
- **Confusing growth with GTM.** Spending money to acquire users who churn is not a GTM strategy. Fix retention first.
