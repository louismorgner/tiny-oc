# Storytelling

Every startup needs a narrative. Not marketing copy — a clear, true story about why this company exists, what it sees that others don't, and where it's going. This narrative shapes everything: who joins, who invests, who buys, and how the team makes decisions.

## The founding narrative

Every good founding story answers: **What did you see that others didn't, and why does it matter?**

This isn't about making your origin sound dramatic. It's about identifying the insight that drove you to start this company.

**Structure:**
1. **The world as it is.** Describe the status quo. What's broken or missing? Be specific.
2. **The insight.** What do you understand about this problem that most people don't? This is the edge.
3. **The vision.** If you're right, what does the world look like? Not in 100 years — in 3-5 years.

**Good founding insight:** "We noticed that every mid-market finance team we talked to was still reconciling data in spreadsheets because existing tools cost $200K+. We realized you could build this for 1/10th the cost with modern infrastructure."

**Bad founding insight:** "We're passionate about making the world a better place through innovative technology."

## Investor narrative

Investors hear hundreds of pitches. The ones that stick follow a specific arc:

### The arc

1. **The world has changed.** Something is different now (technology shift, regulatory change, behavioral shift) that creates an opening. This is not about your company — it's about the world.
2. **This creates a problem (or opportunity).** Because of this change, a specific group of people/companies now has a problem they didn't have before, or an opportunity they couldn't capture before.
3. **Current solutions fail.** Here's what people do today, and here's why it doesn't work (too expensive, too slow, too complex, didn't exist).
4. **We have a unique insight.** Here's what we understand that others don't. This is your technical edge, your market insight, your unfair advantage.
5. **Proof it works.** Traction, metrics, customer love — whatever evidence you have.
6. **The future.** If we're right, here's how big this gets.

### Common narrative mistakes

- Starting with your product instead of the problem
- Making the problem sound theoretical instead of showing a real person suffering from it
- Claiming a huge market without showing the path from here to there
- "We're the Uber for X" — analogies are crutches, not arguments

## Customer narrative

Different from the investor narrative. Customers don't care about your vision or your market size. They care about their problem.

### Structure

1. **You have this problem.** Name it specifically. Make them feel recognized.
2. **Here's why it's hard.** Show you understand the nuance. This builds trust.
3. **There's a better way.** Introduce your solution in terms of the outcome, not the features.
4. **Here's proof.** Case studies, metrics, testimonials. Social proof from people like them.
5. **Here's what to do next.** One clear action.

## Internal narrative

The story you tell your team. Most founders underestimate how much this matters.

### Why it matters

A 5-person team doesn't need OKRs. They need to understand *why* they're building what they're building well enough to make good decisions independently. The internal narrative provides that context.

### What it includes

- **Where we are.** Honest assessment of current state. Don't sugarcoat.
- **Where we're going.** The 6-12 month target, in concrete terms.
- **Why it matters.** What changes if we get there? For customers, for the company, for the team.
- **What we're betting on.** The 1-2 things that have to go right.

### When to update

- After a significant decision or pivot
- After a fundraise
- Quarterly at minimum
- Whenever you notice the team is confused about priorities

## Principles

- **True beats clever.** The best narratives are honest. Don't manufacture drama or insight that isn't there.
- **Specific beats abstract.** "Maria spends 6 hours every week copying data between spreadsheets" > "Companies lose productivity due to fragmented workflows."
- **One idea per story.** If you're trying to communicate three things, you'll communicate zero.
- **Show, then tell.** Lead with the evidence, follow with the interpretation. A demo is better than a description. A metric is better than a claim.
